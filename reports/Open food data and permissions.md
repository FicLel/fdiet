# Replace gated tables, archive the open ones

fdiet's current data is mostly on firm ground, and its one real legal limit is BEDCA, not the sources it has left out. The branded catalogue (`fooddata.csv`) is AESAN's 2022 open-data file, reusable commercially with attribution and an update date. AESAN 2022 and AESAN/MEC 2010 fall under the same public-sector reuse terms. 5 al día is CC BY-SA 4.0 and the USDA yields are public domain. BEDCA alone is **non-commercial and may not be modified**, and while it is in the app it makes the whole app non-commercial. Every source the project has set aside needs a written licence from a named holder: SENC 2018, DIAL, the FINUT photo guide, the Russolillo exchange lists, the Murillo and Sant Joan de Déu diabetes tables, Moreiras, TABULA, AEP 2018 and FAO/INFOODS beyond non-commercial use. ASPCAT's CC BY-NC-ND sits in a grey zone. Open replacements exist for nearly all of them. **CIQUAL 2025 and BLS 4.0** (both CC BY 4.0) can replace BEDCA's figures. **USDA FoodData Central** (CC0) supplies portion weights, refuse factors and densities. **Fineli** (CC BY 4.0) ships household measures. **Open Food Facts** (ODbL) extends the branded catalogue, and **RENHyD** articles (CC BY-SA 4.0) are a minable Spanish source. None of these has Spanish food names or Spanish household measures, so each needs a curated crosswalk to BEDCA, which fdiet's "the machine offers, the nutritionist decides" design already allows for. The repo protects itself against link rot with three layers: verbatim CSVs in per-source folders with a hashed manifest, Wayback captures of every data page *and every licence page*, and a monthly check that flags dead links, upstream changes and unrecorded local edits. The licence pages are already rotting. AESAN's old legal notice, which the repo cites, now returns 404.

## Four of the six loaded sources are clean; BEDCA sets the ceiling

The branded catalogue has a better provenance story than the project documents. `fooddata.csv` is an extract of AESAN's **"Datos de composición de alimentos y bebidas comercializados en España en 2022"** (`BasedatosWeb.xlsx`: 31 columns, **29,575 products**, label data collected by Kantar Worldpanel). This was confirmed by downloading the file and matching rows such as `BEKIND BARRA CEREAL CARAMELO ALMENDRA Y SAL` ([AESAN Alimentos y Bebidas](https://www.aesan.gob.es/en/datos-abiertos/alimentos-y-bebidas); [BasedatosWeb.xlsx](https://www.aesan.gob.es/en/dam/jcr:5f9691b8-10dc-4c23-9963-9b4e829308eb/BasedatosWeb.xlsx)). CLAUDE.md's "~100 k rows" is wrong. The CSV has 29,966 physical lines because ingredient lists span several lines, which is about 29.6 k products. The `food_items` columns that "stay null" are exactly the xlsx columns the CSV dropped (`CuotaMercadoTotalEan`, `Fabricante`, `Submarca`, `Tamañoporción`, `Sodio`, `Fibra`), so the richer export the schema waits for is the original file. AESAN's current legal notice allows commercial and non-commercial reuse on three conditions: cite the source, never "desnaturalizar" the content, and state the date of last update ([AESAN Aviso legal](https://www.aesan.gob.es/en/aviso-legal)). The CSV writes commas inside categories as ";", and nothing records that change. Changing a delimiter does not alter the meaning, but a `FOODDATA-ATTRIBUTION.txt` should say so, name Kantar as the collector, and carry AESAN's disclaimer that the data reflects 2022 labels.

AESAN-2022-007 and the AESAN/MEC 2010 consensus fall under the same notice, which comes from Spain's public-sector reuse regime: Ley 37/2007 and the general AGE conditions ([datos.gob.es aviso legal](https://datos.gob.es/es/aviso-legal); [Ley 37/2007](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-2007-19814)). The regime has two limits. First, Ley 37/2007 excludes documents in which **third parties hold IP**, so a figure AESAN reproduces from SENC or Moreiras is not freed by AESAN's notice. Second, the URL the repo cites for the notice (`/AECOSAN/web/aecosan_aviso_legal.htm`) **returns 404**. The live notice is at `aesan.gob.es/aviso-legal`, and a Wayback capture of the old page exists from 2025-12-07 ([old notice](https://www.aesan.gob.es/AECOSAN/web/aecosan_aviso_legal.htm); [Wayback availability](https://archive.org/wayback/available?url=aesan.gob.es/AECOSAN/web/aecosan_aviso_legal.htm)). `reference-data/sources.csv` still marks both AESAN rows "pendiente de volver a verificar". That can now be closed by pointing at the new URL and its archived copy. The AESAN/MEC 2010 row also takes its PDF from a third-party mirror (seghnp.org), which is exactly the kind of link that disappears.

BEDCA is the binding constraint. Its terms, unchanged since v1.0 (2010), allow reproduction with the attribution "AESAN/BEDCA Base de Datos Española de Composición de Alimentos v1.0 (2010)". Values may not be modified, and "la reproducción, traducción o cualquier uso que no sea personal, educacional o no comercial, de los datos en formato electrónico" needs AESAN/BEDCA's express authorisation ([BEDCA UsoBD.pdf](https://www.bedca.net/bdpub/UsoBD.pdf)). fdiet complies today: values are stored as published with their units, anything derived is computed on read, and the attribution appears in the footer. That compliance holds only while the project is non-commercial. There is **no newer or open BEDCA**. A datos.gob.es request for a CSV/XML release, filed 2026-04-14 and backed by about 67 supporters, is "Assigned" to AESAN with no answer as of 2026-09-29. It even mentions the unofficial GitHub CSV fdiet copied ([datos.gob.es request](https://datos.gob.es/en/solicitud-de-datos/base-de-datos-bedca)). 5 al día (CC BY-SA 4.0, [RENHyD 628](https://www.renhyd.org/renhyd/article/view/628)) is handled correctly. fdiet added a `bedca_food_id` column, which makes the file adapted material, and keeps it ShareAlike with the changes listed in its `LICENSE.md`, as CC BY-SA 4.0 §3 requires ([CC BY-SA 4.0 legal code](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)).

## Twelve gated sources, and the exact party to ask for each

The legal frame decides which sources need permission. A single figure such as "1 cucharada sopera = 10 ml" is a fact and nobody owns it. A whole published table is protected twice in Spain: by copyright in the original selection and arrangement, and by the **sui generis database right** (LPI arts. 133–137, Directive 96/9/EC). That right lets the maker forbid extraction of a substantial part, and forbids repeated systematic extraction of insubstantial parts, for 15 years from completion ([Iberley art. 133](https://www.iberley.es/legislacion/articulo-133-ley-propiedad-intelectual); [BOE LPI](https://www.boe.es/buscar/act.php?id=BOE-A-1996-8930)). The CJEU limits the right to investment in *obtaining, verifying or presenting* data rather than creating it ([C-203/02 BHB v William Hill](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/legal-content/EN/TXT/?uri=celex%3A62002CJ0203)). Measured portion weights are "obtained and verified", so a measured table is almost certainly protected. The text-and-data-mining exceptions (LPI art. 67, DSM Directive art. 4) allow copying for analysis but **not** publishing the copy, so they never cover committing a table to a public repo ([Directive 2019/790](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/dir/2019/790/oj/eng); [RDL 24/2021](https://www.boe.es/diario_boe/txt.php?id=BOE-A-2021-17910)). Silence on a PDF means all rights reserved. A copy circulating on another website is not a licence.

Two Spanish sources need no permission, though they look as if they might. The **ENALIA photographic atlas** (AESAN, 2014) is AESAN's own work, so its portion weights fall under the AESAN reuse notice ([Scribd copy](https://www.scribd.com/document/351723499/Atlas-Fotogr-Fico-Enalia)). The atlas's photographs should still be checked for third-party image rights, and the original belongs on aesan.gob.es, not Scribd. The UCM **Aparicio & Perea 2015** ration and household-measure tables do need permission, although UCM hosts them as a free PDF. They are Appendix XII of a commercial Panamericana book, carry no licence, and their authors call the values "orientativos" ([UCM tablas](https://www.ucm.es/idinutricion/tablas-de-raciones-de-alimentos-y-medidas-caseras)). Nutrición Hospitalaria's CC BY-NC-SA 4.0 would bring the same NC limit as BEDCA into anything derived from it ([Nutrición Hospitalaria](https://www.nutricionhospitalaria.org/)). The Latin American tables carry no licence at all, which leaves them in a worse position than BEDCA's explicit grant ([FAO INFOODS Latin America](https://www.fao.org/infoods/infoods/tables-and-databases/latin-america/en/)). Peru's TAFERA 2016 is an exception worth checking: it has 1,027 household measures with gross and net weights, the right shape for fdiet, but its licence is unverified ([TAFERA PDF](https://cdn.www.gob.pe/uploads/document/file/1427367/TAFERA%202016%20VF.pdf.pdf)).

Asking costs one email. That email should produce a usable licence rather than a friendly "sí". Both BEDCA and TABULA say their conditions "se remitirán a los interesados", so they expect a request that describes the use. The research drafted a Spanish request covering six points:

1. Storing and redistributing the extracted table in a public repo, and whether the source PDF may be stored too.
2. Showing the figures to professionals and patients.
3. Non-commercial use now, with the question asked outright whether a future paid hosted service is covered.
4. Values left unaltered, with derived figures (kJ→kcal, scaling, BEDCA links, counts of units) labelled as fdiet's own.
5. The exact attribution string they want.
6. The duration of the grant, how it can be revoked, and whether they would prefer the data released under CC BY 4.0.

The request should name the table and its pages, the number of rows, the repo URL and fdiet's licence. Since a repo is public, it should also state plainly that anyone who clones it receives the file. Ask for a written, non-exclusive, royalty-free grant.

## Open replacements cover every gap except Spanish wording

For composition, five generic European tables now allow commercial use with attribution only. **CIQUAL 2025** (ANSES) has 3,484 foods and 74 components under CC BY 4.0 and Etalab 2.0, in XML/XLSX with an English version, published 2025-11-19 ([Zenodo 17550133](https://zenodo.org/records/17550133)). **BLS 4.0** (Max Rubner-Institut) has 7,140 foods and 138 nutrients. It dropped its licence fee on 2025-12-15 and is now CC BY 4.0 with MRI named as publisher ([blsdb.de/download](https://blsdb.de/download); [heise](https://www.heise.de/en/news/Bundeslebensmittelschluessel-4-0-Nutrient-database-now-publicly-available-11123877.html)). **Fineli** (CC BY 4.0) has 4,232 foods, ships CSV packages, and is the only European table confirmed to include household measures and portion sizes ([Fineli open data](https://fineli.fi/fineli/en/avoin-data); [git mirror](https://github.com/theel0ja/fineli-data)). **Livsmedelsdatabasen** (CC BY 4.0) is API-only and carries FoodEx2 and LanguaL codes ([Livsmedelsverket](https://www.livsmedelsverket.se/en/about-us/open-data/food-composition-data/)). **Matvaretabellen** is under NLOD 2.0 ([data.norge.no](https://data.norge.no/en/datasets/9d082918-e3d4-4ae2-8efd-e7d025dfd52d/matvaretabellen)). **CoFID** is under OGL v3 ([gov.uk](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/composition-of-foods-integrated-dataset-cofid)). **USDA FoodData Central** is CC0, and USDA asks only for credit, which it does not require ([FDC API guide](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/api-guide/)). Three tables are "ask first" because of extra conditions. NEVO's "only if unchanged" clause sits uneasily with fdiet's derived-on-read figures ([RIVM](https://www.rivm.nl/en/dutch-food-composition-database/use-of-nevo-online/copyright-and-disclaimer)). Frida's commercial terms are unverified ([Frida](https://frida.fooddata.dk/data)). Portugal's INSA TCA is free to access but names no licence ([INSA](https://www.insa.min-saude.pt/disponivel-nova-versao-da-tabela-de-composicao-de-alimentos/)).

CIQUAL is the best first fallback for BEDCA. Its foods are Mediterranean-adjacent, French names translate to Spanish easily (laitue → lechuga, pain de mie → pan de molde), and its 2025 revision reworked breads, cheeses and processed meats ([ANSES 2025](https://ciqual.anses.fr/cms/en/2025-anses-ciqual-table)). BLS is broader but more Central European. Replacing BEDCA outright has a real cost: figures analysed in Spain become French or German ones, and that matters for jamón serrano, chorizo and regional breads. Keep BEDCA while the project is non-commercial. Add CIQUAL beside it as a separately attributed table now, so a switch is a configuration change rather than a migration on the day commercial use arrives. Two facts about CIQUAL and BLS remain unverified and decide how much work the switch takes: whether either file carries an edible-portion factor, which `PortionScaler` depends on, and whether either carries FoodEx2 codes, which would make a semi-automatic BEDCA crosswalk possible. A mapping spike against example-ui.xlsx's 210 ingredients would answer both.

USDA public-domain data replaces DIAL, FINUT and SENC for **weights**. FNDDS 2021–2023 publishes about 22,000 portion weights ([FNDDS fact sheet](https://www.ars.usda.gov/ARSUserFiles/80400530/pdf/fndds/FNDDS_2021_2023_factsheet.pdf)), and SR Legacy is the final, frozen Standard Reference release ([FDC downloads](https://fdc.nal.usda.gov/download-datasets/)). Its `food_portion` table ("1 medium apple", "1 slice", "1 tbsp") and its refuse percentages, which can fill BEDCA's missing edible-portion factors, come from background knowledge rather than a verified schema. Volume-to-weight pairs in the same table give a public-domain substitute for the non-commercial FAO density database. USDA's Retention Factors Release 6 complements the yields fdiet already uses ([USDA ARS](https://www.ars.usda.gov/northeast-area/beltsville-md-bhnrc/beltsville-human-nutrition-research-center/methods-and-application-of-food-composition-laboratory/mafcl-site-pages/nutrient-retention-factors/)). The catch is that the units are American. A US cup is 240 ml and is not a Spanish *taza*, so each row must be mapped by a person to a BEDCA food and a `HouseholdMeasure`, and stored as a `ref_food_measures` row with a source and a point weight. **Spanish wording and Spanish ration conventions** have no open replacement. That gap is closed in three ways: a SENC or DIAL licence, RENHyD CC BY-SA articles mined table by table ([RENHyD about](https://www.renhyd.org/renhyd/about)), or the nutritionist's own global criteria (V14). Those criteria may cite where she cross-checked a value but must never rebuild a gated table row by row.

For branded products, **Open Food Facts** is the realistic open addition. Spain has 372,089 products, crowd-sourced and uneven in quality ([OFF España](https://es.openfoodfacts.org/)). The data is ODbL 1.0, with contents under DbCL and images under CC BY-SA 3.0 ([OFF terms](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/terms-of-use)). Load it from the CSV, JSONL or Parquet dumps rather than the API, which allows 15 product reads per minute and blocks scraping ([OFF data](https://world.openfoodfacts.org/data); [OFF API](https://openfoodfacts.github.io/openfoodfacts-server/api/)). USDA Branded is CC0 but covers US products. GS1 Spain's GDSN is a members-only exchange, and TABULA mirrors BEDCA's restrictions ([GS1 Spain](https://www.gs1es.org/compartir-estandares-gs1/gdsn/)). Store no product images: pack designs are copyrighted whatever the data licence says.

## Containment keeps each licence inside its own folder

Different licences can sit in one repo and one database because both ODbL and CC 4.0 treat datasets kept side by side, unmodified, as a **collection**. Share-alike only spreads to an *adaptation* ([ODbL 1.0](https://opendatacommons.org/licenses/odbl/1-0/); [CC FAQ](https://creativecommons.org/faq/)). The rules follow from that. Each source keeps its own folder and file, and a `source_code` on every row. A file where fdiet adds columns to a source's rows stays under that source's licence, with the changes listed. Better still, the source file stays pristine and fdiet's joins live in a separate `links.csv`. The ODbL exception: a publicly used joined table is still a Derivative Database and must itself be ODbL. Two pairings are impossible. **CC BY-SA and ODbL rows can never be merged into one derived table**, because BY-SA 4.0's only approved compatible licences are GPLv3 and Free Art 1.3 ([CC compatible licenses](https://creativecommons.org/share-your-work/licensing-considerations/compatible-licenses/)). BEDCA cannot be adapted into anything at all. Merging OFF rows into `food_items` by EAN would make that table ODbL, which is acceptable for the AESAN half but must never touch BEDCA.

Attribution comes out of the manifest instead of being written by hand in four places. CC needs title, author, source, licence and a note of modifications ([CC BY-SA 4.0 §3](https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by-sa/4.0/legalcode.en)). OGL needs "Contains public sector information licensed under the Open Government Licence v3.0." ([OGL v3](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/)). Etalab needs the producer and the date of last update ([Etalab 2.0](https://www.data.gouv.fr/pages/legal/licences/etalab-2.0)). Spanish public-sector reuse needs the source and the update date. An ODbL Produced Work, such as a screen of totals, needs a notice naming the database and the licence (§4.3). Generating the UI footer and an attribution report from the same manifest keeps them in step. Every `LICENSE.md` should say that it covers those data files only, as 5 al día's already does, so that nobody reads fdiet's code licence as covering them.

A permission becomes evidence only once it is written down. Each grant goes in `reference-data/<source>/PERMISSION.md`: who granted it (name, role, institution), the date and channel, the request and reply verbatim, and a scope summary (redistribution, commercial, derived values, attribution text, expiry). The grantor's personal email stays out of a public repo unless they agree, because GDPR applies. The manifest then carries `permission_file` and a `LicenseRef-<source>-permission-YYYY-MM-DD` id, and the reference sync refuses to load a source marked permission-required that has no permission file. A refusal or silence is logged with its date, so nobody asks again blindly.

## A three-layer archive turns a 404 into a non-event

Link rot is already happening to the pages that matter most. AESAN's notice moved and the old URL returns 404. Etalab's canonical licence page now redirects to the data.gouv.fr home page ([etalab.gouv.fr](https://www.etalab.gouv.fr/licence-ouverte-open-licence/)). AESAN's July 2025 open-data press release is gone. The first layer is the git repo. Commit the extracted CSV always. Commit the source PDF/XML/XLSX only where the licence allows redistribution (CC0/PD, CC BY, BY-SA, OGL, Etalab, AESAN reuse), using Git LFS or a Zenodo deposit for large files. For BEDCA and permission-gated sources store only a checksum and an archived URL. Software Heritage archives the public repo and gives content-addressed SWHIDs ([Software Heritage](https://www.softwareheritage.org/save-and-reference-research-software/)). The second layer is a Wayback capture of every data URL **and every licence URL**, taken through Save Page Now 2 (`POST web.archive.org/save`, `capture_all=1`) and confirmed through the availability API ([SPN2 docs](https://gist.github.com/regstuff/82e690db2f1d91ba59f6681c1abad6cf)). The archived licence page is the proof of what was granted on the retrieval date. The third layer is a Zenodo deposit with a DOI, for redistributable data only, since Zenodo takes only content the depositor has rights to ([Zenodo policies](https://about.zenodo.org/policies/)).

The manifest records enough to check all of this mechanically. `reference-data/sources.csv` can stay as the index if it gains `licence_id` (SPDX), `licence_url`, `licence_archived_url`, `archived_url`, `original_sha256`, `snapshot_sha256`, `permission_file`, `redistribute_original`, `commercial_use` and `modifications`. Alternatively, a Frictionless `datapackage.json` per folder carries the same fields with a `sha256:` hash and can be validated by `frictionless validate` ([Frictionless Data Resource](https://specs.frictionlessdata.io/data-resource/)). `scripts/check-sources`, run monthly by hand or in CI, runs six checks:

1. Fetch each data and licence URL. A 404, a 410 or a redirect to a home page flags link rot, and nothing is deleted.
2. Hash the upstream file. If it differs from `original_sha256`, flag "upstream changed" for a person to decide; the script never overwrites a file, because BEDCA forbids modification and codes must stay stable.
3. Re-hash each committed CSV. If it differs from `snapshot_sha256`, flag "local edit not recorded", which catches a silent change to BEDCA.
4. Request a Wayback capture where the last one is stale.
5. Validate the package.
6. Print the attribution report.

The SPN2 rate limits and Software Heritage's handling of non-code files were not verified.

## Conclusion

The risk to fdiet's data is uneven, and the project has been guarding the wrong side. Its policy of leaving out SENC, DIAL and FINUT already works. The real exposure is on the open side, where the licence pages for data the project depends on are disappearing, and the branded catalogue's origin was undocumented until this research traced it. Archiving the licence terms with the data, and checking hashes for silent edits, does more for compliance than any single permission email.

One choice is also more urgent than it looks. Commercial use is blocked by one dataset, BEDCA, and the Spanish public-data request for an open BEDCA has gone unanswered for six months. Adding CIQUAL as a parallel, separately attributed table now costs one import and one crosswalk. It turns "go commercial" from a rewrite into a configuration switch, and it does so whether or not AESAN ever answers.
